The world's news, from its source

RSS2

A news archive of your own, to read the world without middlemen

RSS2 gathers the feeds that media outlets publish in their own countries, keeps the original text and translates it on your machine. Then it lets you search, compare and listen: the same event told by several sources, with their accents and their interests in plain sight.

It is not another outlet. It is a tool to read the outlets that already exist, without anyone choosing for you first.

  • Status Active development
  • License MIT (code)
  • Backend Go
  • Data PostgreSQL 16 + pgvector
  • Install Docker or Windows 11

There is no public demo yet: the figures on this page come from an audited lab instance.

Native Windows edition

The Windows 11 x64 installer includes PostgreSQL 16 with pgvector and runs components as Windows services. Use the offline bundle or download components during setup.

The desktop variant opens RSS2 in WebView2. Optional AI components add translation, entity recognition and semantic search. Voice bulletins are still experimental on Windows.

Why it exists

Leaving the echo chamber

Almost everything we know about the world reaches us second-hand. We open an app, see a list of headlines someone picked for us, and end up taking that list for reality. The same topics, the same anger and, often, the same words. It is comfortable. It is also an echo chamber: we hear our own opinion amplified and call it being informed.

The problem is not only the algorithm. It is distance. What happens in another country usually reaches us filtered, summarised and reinterpreted by someone who was not there and who has interests of their own. The original language is lost, the context is lost and, with them, the chance to judge for ourselves.

RSS2 does the opposite: it goes to the source. It brings together thousands of feeds that outlets publish in their own country and language, keeps the original text, translates it on your machine and shows who says what. It does not invent news or rewrite it: it puts the raw material within reach so you can compare, doubt and decide.

A free society does not stand on citizens who repeat headlines. It stands on individuals who can read several versions of the same event, notice what is missing and change their mind when the evidence asks them to. Information is not a product to consume: it is the condition for thinking and choosing for yourself.

That is why RSS2 is free software and runs on your machine. If the tool that filters the world belongs to someone else, so does your gaze. Here the code, the data and the rules are in the open, and you can change them.

What it does

Read, translate, connect and listen

RSS2 does not publish news: it collects it, orders it and hands it back in a form you can query. Everything happens inside your own installation.

  • Reads the sources

    Every fifteen minutes it walks the RSS and Atom feeds of thousands of outlets, resolves relative links and drops duplicates before storing anything.

  • Detects the language

    A worker tags the language of every news item. That decides whether it needs translating and feeds the translation queue.

  • Translates locally

    NLLB-200, served through CTranslate2, translates the content into Spanish on your own CPU or GPU. No cloud API reads what you look up.

  • Connects the pieces

    spaCy extracts people, organisations and places; embeddings group the news that tell the same story and allow search by meaning, not just exact words.

Translating is not interpreting

Machine translation can be wrong, and it is worth knowing that. That is why RSS2 keeps the original text, marks what it has translated and does not rewrite the story. The system does not decide what is true: it brings sources closer, shows their differences and leaves critical reading in your hands.

Search combines semantic similarity with date, topic, country and category filters. You can ask for “what Japan published about nuclear energy this week” and get the original pieces, not somebody's summary.

Pipeline

From feed to related story

RSS/Atom feeds
   │  ingestor (Go, every 15 min)
   ▼
PostgreSQL 16 + pgvector
   │
   ├─ langdetect ──► translation-scheduler ──► translator (NLLB-200)
   ├─ ner (spaCy) ──► entities + co-occurrences
   ├─ embeddings ──► related + event-clustering
   ├─ topics ──► categories and country
   ├─ wiki ──► entity context
   └─ scraper / discovery ──► summaries and new sources
   ▼
Go API :8080 ──► nginx :8888 ──► React SPA

In the Docker edition, Redis caches responses and rate-limits requests, and nginx publishes the SPA and the API on :8888. The Windows edition has no nginx or Redis: server.exe serves the SPA on 127.0.0.1:8080 and the cache lives in memory.

  • Ingestion

    Thousands of sources, no noise

    A Go ingestor walks the feeds every fifteen minutes, normalises addresses and keeps the same article from entering twice.

  • Language

    Automatic detection

    langdetect identifies the language of every item and decides which ones need a translation. Channels already publishing in Spanish are marked separately.

  • Translation

    NLLB-200 on CPU or GPU

    CTranslate2 runs NLLB-200 distilled 600M on your hardware. Translation is local, can be switched off and never replaces the original.

  • Entities

    People, places and links

    spaCy extracts the entities in every text and precomputes their co-occurrences so you can follow a topic across thousands of stories.

  • Search

    By meaning, not by headline

    Multilingual embeddings, indexed with pgvector, let you search in your own words and combine the result with date, topic, country and category.

  • Trends

    One story, several sources

    Similarity clustering groups the five versions of the same story and shows its weight without ranking by outrage.

  • Context

    Wikipedia alongside

    Wikipedia summaries and images linked to the entities that appear in the news, so you do not lose track of who is who.

  • Voice

    Bulletins with a script

    An optional spoken newsreel with editable text, estimated duration and two editorial approvals before anything is generated.

  • Privacy

    Your data stays home

    No third-party accounts and no telemetry. The models run locally and the corpus lives in your PostgreSQL, not on somebody else's server.

  • Install

    Docker or native Windows

    The same code runs in containers or as Windows services with portable PostgreSQL, without Docker, Redis or nginx.

Windows edition

The whole system on a Windows machine, without Docker

The native edition packages what Windows 11 x64 needs: portable PostgreSQL 16 with pgvector, the Go binaries, the AI workers inside a private Python and, if you want it, voice. There is no Docker, Redis or nginx, and no separate Python or PostgreSQL to install.

The installer registers the services with WinSW —RSS2Server, RSS2Postgres, ingestion and the workers—, creates the database in C:\ProgramData\RSS2 and leaves the application listening only on 127.0.0.1:8080. The SPA can open in its own WebView2 window or in the browser. No port is opened on the local network and no firewall rules are created.

The models are installed on the machine —the online Setup downloads them during installation— and then work offline. Search, translation, NER and audio keep working without the Internet; only ingestion, scraping, Wikipedia and source discovery need a network.

  • Prepare the machine

    Windows 11 x64 with Go 1.26+, Node 24+, Visual Studio Build Tools and Inno Setup 6 or 7. preparar-entorno.ps1 checks what is missing and can install it with winget.

  • Build the installer

    windows\build.ps1 compiles the SPA into the binaries, verifies WinSW and PostgreSQL, builds pgvector and prepares the AI pack. The result is RSS2-Setup-<version>.exe.

  • Install and start

    The Setup creates the services, applies the schema to an empty database and waits for /health to answer. The first user to register becomes the administrator.

  • Check and manage

    verify.ps1 checks services, database and SPA. From the /admin/workers panel you can start, stop and restart services, watch CPU, RAM and disk, and detect an NVIDIA GPU.

# on a Windows 11 x64 machine with Go, Node, MSVC and Inno Setup
.\preparar-entorno.ps1 -Instalar
.\windows\build.ps1 -WebViewCab $cab -WebViewSha256 $sha256 -WithTTS

# online installer: ~2 MB + downloadable components with SHA-256
.\windows\build-online.ps1 -Version 1.0.0 -BaseUrl https://my-cdn/rss2/1.0.0

Approximate sizes: core without AI ~65 MB; core with AI ~2.2 GB; desktop with AI ~2.6 GB; with voice ~5.5-7 GB. The online Setup downloads the components with caching and resume, and verifies their hash. The repository's windows.yml workflow builds the installer, tests it on the runner and publishes the .exe with its SHA-256.

What to know before installing

  • Code signing: the .exe is not signed yet; Windows may show a SmartScreen warning.
  • Desktop: the native window needs the WebView2 x64 runtime, which can ship inside the installer or come from the system.
  • Voice: TTS on Windows is experimental. If the build cannot get the native wheels, the app installs without voice and everything else works.
  • A single machine: the cache is in memory and there is no Redis, replication or remote-worker. To scale, use the Docker edition.
  • No MSI: it produces an Inno Setup .exe; GPO or Intune deployment would need an MSI wrapper.

Voice bulletin

A spoken newsreel with editorial control

The voice subsystem is optional and turns the news into a bulletin: you search, choose, fix the text and generate the audio. Everything happens in the web app, and nothing is published automatically.

  • Search and choose by keyword or trend, with a time window, relevance ordering and one item per story.
  • Review the full script of every item, with characters and estimated duration.
  • Process: the edits are saved, the bulletin is created and the narration is queued asynchronously.

Two approval gates

  • The editor approves the content and generates the narrations.
  • An administrator approves the assembled audio; there is no publication endpoint.

Voices: Chatterbox by default, the Spanish base voice, 256 model speakers, OpenVoice or a clone registered from a sample (administrators only). Speed from 0.7x to 1.5x. On CPU narration runs at ~1.5x real time.

Status

Audit of a lab instance

The latest published audit (12 October 2026) measured the full system with every service healthy:

  • 1,284,617 news items and 28,704 registered sources.
  • 195 countries, 15 categories and 194,324 translations.
  • 375,120 entity co-occurrences precomputed for the graph.
  • /api/stats in ~50 ms and the entity graph in ~200 ms.
  • The Windows edition was installed and verified on a Windows 11 virtual machine on 24 November 2026: 12 services, verify.ps1 clean and RSS2.exe --smoke-test returning 0.
  • CI green on the latest master run: Go tests with vet and gofmt, React tests and build, and the init-db schema from scratch with drift checks.

Known limits

  • The MIT licence covers the code: the NLLB-200 weights are CC-BY-NC 4.0 and restrict commercial use.
  • The backend takes ~5 minutes to start because of indexes and statistics on large tables.
  • TTS on CPU runs at ~1.5x real time; the queue is asynchronous.
  • No public demo and no tagged releases: the figures come from a lab instance.
  • Part of the corpus has corrupt dates and few recent items: the 24 h window may return nothing.

Get started

Two ways to run it

With Docker on any Linux or macOS machine that has Compose, or as native services on Windows 11. Either way you need about 8 GB of RAM and enough room for the corpus and the models.

With Docker

git clone https://github.com/Dinamicas-Electronicas/rss2.git
cd rss2
cp .env.example .env      # POSTGRES_PASSWORD, REDIS_PASSWORD, SECRET_KEY
docker compose up -d --build

Open http://localhost:8888. The voice bulletin is optional: it needs the model weights via make tts-models.

On Windows, without Docker

Build the installer with windows\build.ps1 and run it as administrator; the Setup creates the services and the portable database. If you want to review the process first, the details are in docs/WINDOWS.md and the checklist in windows/VM-TEST.md.

In the Windows edition open http://127.0.0.1:8080 (or the desktop window). The first user to register becomes the administrator.
